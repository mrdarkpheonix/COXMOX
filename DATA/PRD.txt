1. OVERVIEW
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Product name : Earth Jukebox (working title; rename if you wish)
One-liner    : See Earth change. Hear Earth change.
Summary      : A web app that shows NASA Earth Information Center (EIC)
               style visuals and plays a live-generated sound for the same
               data, so people can understand Earth science through
               sight AND sound, including people who cannot see the screen.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CHALLENGE STATEMENT (as quoted by a third-party page) [VERIFY]
---------------------------------------------------------------------
"NASA's Earth Information Center (EIC) produces stunning visualizations of
our changing planet, but presenting Earth science through visualization
alone limits who can reach it. The challenge is to build an 'Earth
Jukebox' - an interface, script, or application that pairs EIC visual
frames with dynamic sonifications generated in real time. The goal is to
make Earth science accessible, engaging, and multi-sensory by translating
sight into sound."

---------------------------------------------------------------------
3. PROBLEM ANALYSIS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 What the challenge really asks (broken into parts)
  a) PAIR     - show an EIC-style visual frame together with a sound.
  b) DYNAMIC  - the sound must come from the data, not a fixed recording.
  c) REAL TIME- sound is generated live in the app (not pre-rendered).
  d) ACCESSIBLE - reach people who a visualization alone leaves out.
  e) MULTI-SENSORY / ENGAGING - it should be pleasant and interesting.
  The deliverable can be "an interface, script, or application", so a web
  app is fully in scope.

3.2 Who is left out today
  - Blind and low-vision people (charts and maps are inaccessible)
  - People with colour-vision differences
  - People who learn better by listening
  - Students, teachers, and the general public who find science images
    hard to interpret

3.3 What a winning answer likely needs (my analysis, not NASA text)
  - Real NASA data driving the sound (judges check "validity").
  - A clear, explained mapping: data value -> sound (see section 8).
  - Works without sight: keyboard, screen reader, spoken description.
  - Works on a phone and is simple enough to demo in 2 minutes.
  - Honest limits: say what is real data and what is artistic.

3.4 Other teams are building similar ideas. To stand out, pick ONE
    clear angle (see section 5) and do it well.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
4. GOALS AND NON-GOALS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
GOALS
  G1. Let a user pick an Earth topic and instantly see AND hear it.
  G2. Generate sound live in the browser from real NASA data.
  G3. Be fully usable with keyboard and screen reader (no mouse, no sight).
  G4. Explain in plain words what the user is hearing.
  G5. Ship a working, deployed demo within the event time.

NON-GOALS (do not build these now)
  - Official warnings or forecasting (this is education and access).
  - Accounts, logins, or a database.
  - A native mobile app (use a responsive web app).
  - Perfect scientific accuracy of every mapping (explain it instead).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5. UNIQUE ANGLE (choose one; recommended = A)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  A. "Instrument per Earth topic" (RECOMMENDED - already prototyped)
     Each topic has its own instrument and music (e.g. wildfire =
     marimba, flood = piano). Data controls pitch, speed, loudness.
  B. "Then vs Now" - hear 1990 and today side by side.
  C. "My region" - hear changes where the user lives.
  Pick A for the core; add B as a bonus if time allows.

6. USERS AND SCENARIOS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  U1. Asha (blind student): opens the app with a screen reader, picks
      "Sea ice" with the keyboard, hears bells thin out as ice shrinks,
      and hears a spoken summary.
  U2. Ravi (teacher): projects the app, plays the tour for a class.
  U3. Meera (curious public): taps a topic on her phone, sees the
      NASA-style image, and hears it.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
7. FEATURES AND PRIORITY
---------------------------------------------------------------------
MUST HAVE (MVP)  - build these first
  F1. Topic picker: 6-8 topics (see section 8), big buttons.
  F2. Visual panel: NASA imagery or a chart for the chosen topic.
  F3. Live sonification: Web Audio / Tone.js plays sound from data.
  F4. Play / Pause / Stop, volume, and a "speed" or severity control.
  F5. Plain-language caption: what you see and what you hear.
  F6. Text-to-speech description (browser speechSynthesis).
  F7. Full keyboard support + screen-reader labels (ARIA live region).
  F8. Data and credits panel: dataset names, links, "not official NASA".

SHOULD HAVE
  F9.  Time slider (years/months) so sound and image change together.
  F10. Sync: moving the slider updates image, sound, and caption.
  F11. Live NASA event counts (EONET) shown on topic cards.
  F12. Offline-safe demo data if the network or API fails.
  F13. Light and dark themes; adjustable text size.

NICE TO HAVE
  F14. "Then vs Now" comparison mode.
  F15. Voice commands (Chrome/Edge only).
  F16. "My region" using the browser location, with permission.
  F17. Export a short audio clip or share link.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
8. SONIFICATION DESIGN (data -> sound)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Rules to keep it understandable:
  - Never use loudness to carry data value (hard to compare, can hurt
    ears). Use pitch, rhythm, density, or instrument instead.
  - Same direction everywhere: "more / worse / hotter" = higher pitch or
    denser notes. Say this on screen.
  - Always show the mapping in a small table.

Starter topics and mappings (data names are suggestions, [VERIFY]):
  Topic            Data source idea        Instrument   Data -> sound
  ---------------  ----------------------  -----------  ----------------
  Global warming   GISTEMP temp anomaly    Trumpet/     warmer = higher
                                           piano        pitch
  Sea ice          NSIDC sea ice extent    Glockenspiel less ice = fewer,
                                                        slower bells
  Wildfires        EONET / FIRMS fires     Marimba      more fires = more
                                                        notes per second
  Floods           EONET / GPM rainfall    Piano        more water = more
                                                        and faster notes
  Storms           EONET storms            Violin       stronger = faster
                                                        tremolo
  Volcanoes        EONET volcanoes         Pipe organ   more activity =
                                                        darker chords
  Drought/Heat     temperature/vegetation  Flute/       drier = thinner,
                   (NDVI)                  clarinet     more silence
  Air quality      NO2 / CO / PM2.5        Clarinet     more pollution =
                                                        murkier, layered
  Note: the working prototype in the earth-jukebox folder already has 10
  composed instruments. Reuse that audio code inside React.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
9. USER FLOW
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Landing: title, one sentence, big "Start listening" button.
  2. Topic picker: cards with icon, name, instrument.
  3. Topic page:
       - top: image/chart  - middle: Play/Pause, slider, volume
       - bottom: caption, mapping table, data source, "Read aloud"
  4. Switch topic any time; sound crossfades or stops cleanly.
  5. About page: data credits, accessibility statement, disclaimer.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
10. UI THEME: SIMPLE AND EASY ACCESS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  - One column on phones, two columns on desktop. Few elements per screen.
  - Large buttons (at least 44 x 44 px), clear labels, no icon-only buttons.
  - High contrast (text contrast at least 4.5:1). Light and dark mode.
  - Calm palette: deep navy background, white text, one accent colour
    (cyan). Never use colour alone to show meaning.
  - Readable font (system sans-serif), base size 18px, user can enlarge.
  - Visible focus ring on every control. Logical tab order.
  - Respect "reduce motion". No flashing or auto-playing sound:
    sound starts ONLY after a user action.
  - Short plain-English text. No jargon without an explanation.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
11. ACCESSIBILITY REQUIREMENTS (target WCAG 2.2 AA)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  A1. Everything works with the keyboard alone.
  A2. Semantic HTML: header, main, nav, buttons, headings in order.
  A3. aria-live region announces topic changes and playback state.
  A4. Every image has meaningful alt text describing the data trend.
  A5. Text description available for every sound (caption + speech).
  A6. A "Skip to content" link. Page language set (lang="en").
  A7. Test with a real screen reader (NVDA / TalkBack / VoiceOver) and
      with the monitor turned off.
  A8. Do not trap focus. Provide a clear Stop button reachable by Tab.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
12. TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS (REACT)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Stack
  - React 18 + Vite (fast dev, easy deploy). Plain CSS or CSS modules.
  - Audio: Web Audio API (raw) or Tone.js. No audio files needed.
  - Data fetch: fetch() to public NASA APIs; cache results in memory.
  - Optional: Leaflet or a static NASA image for the map.
  - Deploy: Netlify or Vercel (free, https).

Suggested structure
  src/
    main.jsx, App.jsx
    components/ TopicCard, TopicPage, Player, Slider, Caption,
                MappingTable, DataCredits, SkipLink
    audio/      engine.js (context, master gain, reverb),
                instruments.js, composers.js
    data/       topics.js (config), api.js (fetch + fallback), demo/*.json
    hooks/      useAudio.js, useSpeech.js, useNasaData.js
    styles/     theme.css

Performance and reliability
  - Create the AudioContext only after a click.
  - Stop all audio on page change / unmount (no leaked oscillators).
  - If an API fails, show a clear message and use bundled demo data.
  - Keep the first load small (under about 1 MB of JS if possible).

Browser support
  - Chrome / Edge (best), Firefox, Safari (voice commands may not work).
  - Location and microphone need https or localhost.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
13. DATA REQUIREMENTS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Use real NASA data wherever possible; label demo data as demo.
  - Show dataset name, date range, and a link on every topic.
  - Normalise values to 0-1 before mapping to sound.
  - See RESOURCES_GUIDE.txt for sources and links.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
14. SUCCESS METRICS (for the demo and judges)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  - A new user hears a data-driven sound within 10 seconds, in 2 clicks.
  - Passes a keyboard-only and a screen-reader walk-through.
  - At least 6 topics, each with a different instrument and real data.
  - Lighthouse accessibility score 95 or higher.
  - Demo video of 1-2 minutes tells the story clearly.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
15. JUDGING ALIGNMENT [VERIFY on the official site]
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Space Apps projects are usually judged on things like impact, creativity,
validity, relevance, and presentation (check the official list).
  Impact     -> helps blind / low-vision people and learners
  Creativity -> an instrument per Earth topic
  Validity   -> real NASA data, mapping explained
  Relevance  -> pairs visuals with live sonification, as asked
  Presentation -> clean demo, clear README, short video

---------------------------------------------------------------------
17. RISKS AND MITIGATIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  - NASA API down or blocked by CORS -> bundled demo data + clear label.
  - Sound is unpleasant or confusing -> keep sounds gentle, test with
    other people, show the mapping.
  - Browser blocks audio -> start only after a click; show a clear button.
  - Too much scope -> finish MVP (F1-F8) before anything else.
  - Looks like other teams -> lead with the instrument-per-topic idea
    and the accessibility story.
  - Over-claiming -> label "not an official NASA product, not a warning
    system".
